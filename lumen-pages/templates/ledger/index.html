From invoice to money in the bank, in four dated steps.

The timeline below is what a real invoice looks like inside Quay. Every step has a date and a rate attached, so your accountant never has to ask.

Day 0

You invoice

Send the invoice from Quay or forward the PDF. We read the amount, currency and due date.

Day 0, same minute

Rate lock

The EUR value is fixed at the mid-market rate plus your plan’s spread. It will not move, whatever the dollar does.

Day 1 to 60

Buyer pays locally

Your buyer pays a domestic account in their own currency, so there is no wire fee and no excuse for a late payment.

Next business day

Settlement

The locked EUR amount lands in your bank. The ledger entry, rate and reference are exported to your books.

Four things you will use every week.

Rate lock up to 180 days

Lock at invoice, or set a trigger rate and let Quay lock when the market touches it. Partial locks are fine for staged deliveries.

Local collection accounts 14 currencies

A US routing number, a UK sort code, a Japanese branch account, all in your company name. Buyers see a domestic payee.

Runway view 90-day

Open receivables, locked value, and expected settlement dates on one screen, netted against the supplier invoices you still owe.

Books export nightly

Netvisor, Xero, Fortnox and QuickBooks. One journal line per settlement, with the realised rate and our fee as separate lines.

A flat fee and a spread you can read.

No account fees, no minimums, no inactivity charges. The spread is the whole cost of a conversion, and it is printed on every ledger line.
